## Space Science

The gap between our theoretical models and observational reality has become starkly apparent in recent studies of cosmic evolution. A massive analysis of 60,000 active galactic nuclei reveals that three leading cosmological simulations fundamentally misrepresent how galaxies and black holes develop within dark matter structures. These computational frameworks, which cost millions to develop and run, struggle particularly with galaxy demographics in different environments—overproducing certain galaxy types while missing others entirely. The findings suggest our understanding of how star formation, black hole activity, and dark matter interact remains incomplete, forcing astronomers to reconsider foundational assumptions about cosmic structure formation.

Meanwhile, the search for life beyond Earth has gained momentum through multiple discoveries. Researchers have demonstrated that hardy Earth organisms could survive asteroid impacts on Mars and the subsequent journey through space, lending credibility to panspermia theories. Simultaneously, astronomers studying the young TOI-2076 planetary system have captured atmospheric evolution in real time, watching as stellar radiation strips away planetary atmospheres at different rates based on orbital distance. These findings provide crucial data points for understanding how potentially habitable worlds develop and whether life could naturally migrate between planets.

NASA's human spaceflight ambitions are accelerating with expanded Artemis program goals that promise sustained lunar presence. After resolving helium system issues that threatened the April timeline, Artemis 2 remains on track while NASA has committed to annual lunar landings starting in 2028. This cadence represents a fundamental shift from Apollo's brief visits to establishing permanent lunar operations, supported by commercial partnerships with SpaceX and Blue Origin that will be tested in the interim Artemis III mission.

- **[Galaxy and black hole co-evolution in dark matter haloes not captured by cosmological simulations](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-026-02792-y)** (Nature Astronomy) — A comprehensive analysis of approximately 60,000 nearby active galactic nuclei and 500,000 host galaxies reveals that three major cosmological simulations (SIMBA, TNG, and EAGLE) fail to accurately reproduce observed relationships between star formation, black hole activity, stellar mass, and dark matter halo properties. The study finds that while simulations qualitatively capture some trends, they significantly misrepresent galaxy demographics in different environments, particularly overproducing quenched low-mass satellite galaxies in massive halos and misrepresenting quenched fractions in low-density regions.

## Space Policy

NASA's dramatic restructuring under Administrator Jared Isaacman reflects a pragmatic shift toward operational reality over ambitious timelines. The Artemis program's pivot to simplified Block 1 configurations and Earth-orbit testing acknowledges technical constraints while pursuing more frequent launches. Simultaneously, the quiet abandonment of the Exploration Upper Stage and Mobile Launcher-2 signals a fundamental reassessment of program scope. The new "NASA Force" talent recruitment initiative suggests recognition that internal engineering capabilities need strengthening to execute these revised plans effectively.

The space industry's operational landscape reveals both resilience and fragility in critical infrastructure. Russia's swift two-month repair of Baikonur's Site 31 demonstrates established space powers' capacity to maintain launch cadence despite infrastructure failures. However, the Pentagon's GHOST-R program introduces concerning ambiguities into space operations. By blending commercial deployment with government control of surveillance assets, the initiative creates gray zones where routine orbital activities could be misinterpreted as hostile actions, potentially escalating tensions in an already crowded geosynchronous environment.
