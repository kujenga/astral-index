## Space Science

The intersection of commercial space capabilities and national security concerns has reached a new inflection point, as demonstrated by Planet Labs' decision to delay satellite imagery releases in conflict zones. Meanwhile, fundamental space science continues advancing our understanding of cosmic evolution. New research using tomographic clustering techniques has revealed that the universe contains twice as much molecular gas as previously detected through galaxy surveys, with implications for how we understand star formation over the past 12 billion years. The finding that molecular gas has a depletion timescale of roughly one billion years suggests continuous gas inflow mechanisms that sustain stellar nurseries across cosmic time.

NASA's operational tempo remains robust, with the agency preparing for another International Space Station resupply mission while simultaneously advancing aeronautics research partnerships. The upcoming Northrop Grumman CRS-24 mission will deliver quantum science equipment and stem cell research hardware to the orbital laboratory, continuing the station's role as humanity's premier microgravity research platform.

## Launch & Propulsion

NASA's decision to swap Boeing's troubled Exploration Upper Stage for ULA's proven Centaur 5 represents a pragmatic shift toward reliability over custom development. The move, effective with Artemis 4 in 2028, reflects Administrator Jared Isaacman's push to streamline SLS operations after years of Boeing's cost overruns and delays. This standardization strategy acknowledges that off-the-shelf solutions often outperform bespoke aerospace hardware, especially when commercial alternatives have matured rapidly.

The global launch industry continues its aggressive expansion, with Spanish startup PLD Space securing $209 million to scale Miura 5 production toward 30 annual flights by 2030. These developments underscore a broader industry transition from government-led programs toward commercial solutions that prioritize rapid iteration, cost efficiency, and manufacturing scale over traditional aerospace approaches.

## International Programs

Europe's space ambitions face both setbacks and progress as ESA grapples with a significant technical challenge while celebrating organizational milestones. The agency has lost contact with the Coronagraph spacecraft in its PROBA-3 mission after an attitude control anomaly left the probe unable to orient its solar arrays toward the Sun. This failure prevents the mission from achieving its primary goal of observing the Sun's corona, though engineers are exploring whether the mission's healthy Occulter spacecraft might help assess the Coronagraph's condition. The incident underscores the persistent risks in complex space operations, even for relatively small demonstration missions.

Meanwhile, ESA's leadership transformation reflects broader changes in the space sector's demographics. Women now comprise 37% of the agency's new recruits and 27% of top management positions, approaching the 40% recruitment target set for 2025. This shift coincides with space technology's expanding terrestrial applications, as food production techniques originally developed for astronauts find new uses in Earth-based agriculture. The Copernicus Sentinel-2 satellites continue documenting rapid urbanization patterns, with recent imagery showing Dhaka's explosive growth consuming agricultural and wetland areas around Bangladesh's capital.

These developments illustrate how international space programs increasingly serve dual purposes: advancing scientific knowledge while addressing practical challenges on Earth, from sustainable agriculture to urban planning in megacities exceeding 36 million residents.
